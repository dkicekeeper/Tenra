How deposit interest is counted

Every month the bank pays interest on the balance: the balance times the annual rate, divided by 12. With compounding the interest joins the deposit, so next month it earns on a larger amount.

Amount = Deposit × (1 + Rate / 12) ^ Months

Without compounding the interest is paid out separately and the deposit does not grow: every month earns the same.

What the effective rate means

The effective rate is what the deposit really earns in a year once compounding is counted. For example, 15% compounded monthly earns 16.08% a year.

Common questions

How does a top-up change the result?

Each top-up starts earning from the next month. The earlier the money goes in, the longer it works.

Is compounding better?

At the same rate a compounding deposit earns more, because interest is paid on earlier interest too. Compare deposits by their effective rate.

Why is my bank's figure slightly different?

Banks count interest by the day and months differ in length. This calculator counts by the month, so the difference is usually a few tenths of a percent.
